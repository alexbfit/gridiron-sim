Methodology

What is compared

Kalshi lists each player stat as a ladder of binary markets ("70+ receiving yards", "80+", …). The mid of the yes bid and ask is the market's probability that the player clears that rung. Sportsbook player props (when a line pull has run — Sunday mornings) are Over/Under pairs; the two prices are de-vigged within each book (p_over / (p_over + p_under)). Anytime-touchdown "Yes" prices are one-sided and get the same 12% vig adjustment the projections use.

Our probability

The simulation stores DraftKings points per sim, not per-stat draws, so each stat gets a parametric distribution around a blended mean. The mean is the sim's expected stat line for the player, pulled 85% toward the sportsbook-implied line when one exists (the same pull the lineup builder uses, which backtested best). When only Kalshi has a line the model is shrunk half-way toward it — the sim alone is known to be weaker than market lines, and ignoring the market would overstate every gap — and the row is marked sim-only. Means are conditional on the player playing (Kalshi settles at the pre-game price if an active player never takes a snap).

Shapes: yardage is lognormal (right-skewed, positive) with a coefficient of variation per market and position; receptions, passing touchdowns and touchdowns are Poisson. The CVs are tunable in jobs/edges.py; the live run also fits a lognormal to each Kalshi ladder and reports the market-implied CV next to the table value so they can be re-tuned.

EV and stake

Kalshi charges a taker fee of about 0.07 × price × (1 − price) per contract (1.75¢ at 50¢), so EV is computed at the ask plus fee, not the mid: EV = p × 1 / (ask + fee) − 1. Sportsbook EV uses the book's decimal price. The stake column is a quarter-Kelly fraction ¼ × (p·b − q) / b, capped at 2% of bankroll; it is a sizing convention on a research screen, not a recommendation.

Known weaknesses

The sim's usage shares lag news (a starter ruled out on Friday, a backfield change), its passing-yard means run low for some quarterbacks, and lognormal tails are thinner than the market's for low-volume receivers. Edges above 25 points are flagged and hidden by default for exactly this reason. Nothing here has been forward-tested against settled markets yet; the Track Record page grades the projections themselves.
