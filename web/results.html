Contest Flashback

The honest scoreboard. After the games, every one of the contest's real entries (from the standings export) is scored in the same simulated worlds as our lineups, so the question is not "did we win Sunday" but "how much would these lineups make on average against these opponents". Consensus view puts every player on the market projection (props / salary line) and judges construction alone — stacks, uniqueness, leverage against what the field actually played. Model view uses our own projections, so it also carries our opinions; over a season its gap to the realized number tells you how much to trust them. The field's own expected ROI is about −15% (the rake); a portfolio that sits above the field's median in the consensus view is built better than the average entry. One week proves nothing.

News agents — did the notes help?

Per-game news readers file sourced role/injury notes Saturday evening and Sunday morning; the lineup task applies the confident ones as overrides. After the games each adjustment is graded: helped = the adjusted projection was closer to the actual than the sim's. Give it several weeks — one Sunday proves nothing.

Upset picks vs the market

For fun and to test a theory: the same agents give a win probability for every game, logged next to the spread-implied market probability. Scored by Brier (lower is better) — if the agents cannot beat the market over a season, the "upset story" idea is dead.
